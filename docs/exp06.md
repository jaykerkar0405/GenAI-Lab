# Aim

To implement a convolutional Variational Autoencoder (VAE) for image generation, and to use it for reconstruction,
sampling, latent-space interpolation and latent-space visualisation.

# Theory

A **Variational Autoencoder** (Kingma and Welling, 2014) is a probabilistic generative model with a latent variable
$z$. An *encoder* network gives an approximate posterior $q_\phi(z|x)=\mathcal{N}(\mu_\phi(x),\sigma^2_\phi(x))$ and a
*decoder* network models $p_\theta(x|z)$. Training maximises the evidence lower bound (ELBO)
$$\log p_\theta(x)\;\ge\;\mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(z|x)}[\log p_\theta(x|z)]-\mathrm{KL}\big(q_\phi(z|x)\,\|\,p(z)\big),\qquad p(z)=\mathcal{N}(0,I).$$
The first term is the reconstruction quality; the KL term (closed form for Gaussians,
$-\tfrac12\sum(1+\log\sigma^2-\mu^2-\sigma^2)$) keeps the latent space close to a standard normal, so *new images can
be generated by sampling $z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)$ and decoding it*. Gradients flow through the random sampling thanks to the
**reparameterisation trick** $z=\mu+\sigma\odot\epsilon$. Weighting the KL term by $\beta$ ($\beta$-VAE) trades
reconstruction sharpness against a smoother, more disentangled latent space. Compared to GANs, VAEs train stably and
give an explicit latent space, but their samples are usually blurrier.

# Steps

1. Load MNIST and scale the pixels to $[0,1]$.
2. Build the encoder (two strided convolutions, dense layer, $\mu$ and $\log\sigma^2$ heads), the sampling layer and the
   transposed-convolution decoder.
3. Implement the ELBO loss in a custom `train_step` and train a 16-dimensional VAE for 15 epochs.
4. Plot the loss components, reconstruct unseen test digits, sample new digits from the prior and interpolate between
   two real digits.
5. Train a 2-D VAE and plot the encoded test digits and the decoded latent manifold.
6. Train VAEs with $\beta\in\{0.1,1,4\}$ and compare the reconstruction/KL trade-off.

# Conclusion

The VAE learned a smooth, structured latent space of handwritten digits. After 15 epochs the 16-dimensional model reached a
negative ELBO of 99.3 nats (reconstruction 76.6 + KL 22.7) and a test reconstruction MSE of 0.0092; reconstructions of unseen digits were
faithful, digits sampled from the prior were recognisable (but blurrier than GAN samples), and interpolations changed gradually from one digit to
another. The 2-D VAE had a higher loss (144.9) because of its tiny bottleneck, but it arranged the digit classes in clusters whose decoded
manifold shows continuous morphing between digits. Varying $\beta$ showed the expected trade-off: from $\beta=0.1$ to $\beta=4$ the reconstruction
loss rose from 68.8 to 111.2 while the KL term fell from 48.2 to 9.1, i.e. a stronger KL weight gives a more regular latent space at the cost
of blurrier reconstructions.
